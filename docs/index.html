// 01 -- Event overview

What happened that night

On the evening of March 13, 1997, something crossed the sky above the American Southwest. Starting near Henderson, Nevada just before 8pm local time, and traveling southeast through Arizona over the next two and a half hours, it was observed by over a thousand independent witnesses -- including airline pilots, police officers, military personnel, and the governor of Arizona himself.

It made no sound. It appeared as a massive V-shaped formation of lights estimated by multiple credentialed witnesses to span over a mile across. It blocked out the stars as it passed overhead. Near Tucson, it was gone.

The US Air Force's official explanation: flares dropped by A-10 Warthog aircraft during a training exercise. That explanation has significant problems -- four of eight tracked speed segments exceeded the A-10's maximum of 706 km/h, and flares do not disturb magnetometers in Colorado 1,013 km away.

On the methodology: everything in this report comes from publicly available government data -- USGS magnetometer archives, GFZ Potsdam geomagnetic indices, and NOAA NEXRAD radar records. Every finding can be independently reproduced. Source code at github.com/Beastros/emf-anomaly-research.

// 02 -- The two-station discovery

One thousand kilometers apart. Moving in lockstep.

The most striking finding is not the spike in Tucson. It is what happened simultaneously 1,013 kilometers away in Boulder, Colorado.

We compared the Tucson magnetometer against the Boulder magnetometer minute-by-minute during the event. Two completely independent government instruments in different states with their own atomic clocks and no connection to each other. Their D-field readings correlated at 0.9704 out of 1.0 for the entire 70-minute window. Time lag between them: one minute -- essentially simultaneous. Implied propagation speed: over 60,000 km/h.

That is not a physical object moving through air. It means whatever caused this was either large enough to affect both states simultaneously, or generated a field that propagated at near-electromagnetic speeds.

Figure 1. Multi-station magnetometer wavefront analysis. Second panel: TUC (red) and BOU (green) D-field normalized -- two lines in near-perfect unison across 1,013 km during the event window. Red vertical line marks 03:32 UTC -- last reported sighting, moment of the 6.333-sigma spike. No eyewitness timestamps were used in this calculation.

This is the key finding. Two independent government instruments in different states showing 0.9704 correlation during a specific 70-minute window on a geomagnetically quiet night. Stations in Alaska, Hawaii, and Virginia showed nothing comparable -- confirming this was localized to the Arizona-Colorado corridor, not a solar event.

// 05 -- Honest limits

What we can and cannot conclude

The findings are real and reproducible. Rigorous analysis requires stating what the data does not prove.

Classified military activity. The US military operates classified aircraft and electronic warfare systems that generate strong EM fields. We cannot access classified flight records. This is the most credible conventional explanation -- though it struggles to account for simultaneous 0.97 correlation across 1,013 km on a quiet night.

Unusual ionospheric event. Traveling ionospheric disturbances can produce multi-station magnetic signatures. However these are associated with solar activity -- which was confirmed absent. A spontaneous large-amplitude event during perfectly quiet conditions coinciding exactly with the sighting window would itself require explanation.

Witness timestamp uncertainty. Velocity calculations are derived from human-reported times and locations. These carry inherent uncertainty. The speed figures are estimates. The segments showing above-A-10 speeds could shift with different timestamp assumptions.

What the data clearly shows: the A-10 flare explanation is inconsistent with the magnetometer record. Flares do not disturb magnetometers in Colorado. The instruments registered something that the official explanation does not account for.
