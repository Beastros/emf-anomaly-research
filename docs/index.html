EMF Anomaly Research — Public Report — 2026-04-30

Cross-Sensor Analysis of Geomagnetic and Ionospheric Data During Documented Anomalous Events

Open-source analysis using public government sensor archives. All findings are reproducible from source data. Code and raw outputs available in the linked repository.

Code: github.com/Beastros/emf-anomaly-research
Contact: emfproj@proton.me

Abstract. This project applies a multi-sensor anomaly detection framework to publicly archived government sensor data, cross-correlated against dates and locations of well-documented aerial events. For each event, magnetometer data from independent USGS and INTERMAGNET stations is retrieved, scored against a rolling baseline, and evaluated under a solar activity gate using the GFZ Kp index. Cross-station Pearson correlation identifies whether independent instruments responded simultaneously.

The primary case examined is the Phoenix Lights event of March 13, 1997. Two independent USGS magnetometer stations separated by 1,013 km produced a Pearson correlation of 0.9704 during the event window under geomagnetically quiet conditions (Kp = 2.0). A subsequent spatial analysis pulled data from six additional stations across Alaska, Hawaii, Virginia, Puerto Rico, and the Arctic — all of which were flat during the event window. A parallel ionospheric TEC analysis using IONEX data found no anomaly at any of four post-1998 events, consistent with a localized magnetic disturbance rather than a broad ionospheric or solar phenomenon.

The same two-station correlation pattern was reproduced independently on the USS Nimitz date (2004, r = 0.978) and the O'Hare Airport date (2006, r = 0.9796). Whatever produced this signal at each event: affected the geomagnetic field simultaneously across distances exceeding 1,000 km; was confined spatially to a regional corridor rather than a global field; and left no detectable ionospheric signature. No conventional single-source explanation has been identified that accounts for all three characteristics together.

Method

Anomaly Convergence Score

Sensor data is retrieved from public APIs for a ±2-hour window around each event. Each data stream is scored against a rolling 20-minute baseline. The GFZ Potsdam Kp index acts as a solar gate: readings during elevated geomagnetic activity (Kp ≥ 3.0) are down-weighted by a factor of 5. A convergence event is logged when two or more independent sensor streams exceed 2.0σ within a ±10-minute window.

ACS(t) = ∑ [ w⊂n × σ⊂n(t) × G(Kp) × N(s,t) ] σ⊂n(t) = |x − mean(20-min window)| / std(20-min window) G(Kp) = 1.0 if Kp < 3.0 — solar ELIMINATED 0.2 if Kp ≥ 3.0 — solar POSSIBLE Weights : magnetometer 1.0 | NEXRAD 0.8 | Kp index 0.6 Threshold: σ > 2.0 to flag

Cross-station propagation velocity is estimated as station separation divided by time lag at peak Pearson correlation. Spatial extent is assessed by pulling additional stations from the INTERMAGNET HAPI archive and checking whether each falls above or below the 2.0σ threshold during the event window.

Finding 02 — Two-station magnetometer lockstep

Independent magnetometers at Tucson AZ and Boulder CO — 1,013 km apart — moved in near-perfect synchrony during the event window under quiet solar conditions.

USGS fluxgate magnetometers at Tucson, AZ (TUC) and Boulder, CO (BOU) operate on independent data acquisition systems with independent timing. During the Phoenix Lights window, their D-component (east-west magnetic field) traces produced a Pearson correlation of 0.9704 at a lag of −1 minute. At 1-minute data resolution, the implied propagation velocity across the 1,013 km station separation exceeds 60,000 km/h. Solar activity that evening was Kp = 2.0, well below the 3.0 threshold used to gate solar explanations.

Supplemental Analysis — Phoenix 1997

Spatial Extent of the Magnetometer Anomaly

Following the TUC-BOU correlation finding, data was pulled from six additional INTERMAGNET stations spanning Alaska, Hawaii, Virginia, Puerto Rico, and the Arctic. The working question: was the 03:32 UTC anomaly confined to the Arizona–Colorado corridor, or present across a wider geographic area?

A broader anomaly footprint would be consistent with a large-scale geomagnetic disturbance affecting the region generally. A confined footprint — where only stations along the corridor are elevated and distant stations are flat — would be spatially consistent with a localized source.

This rules out a global geomagnetic storm, a broad ionospheric disturbance, and any mechanism that would be expected to affect stations continent-wide or globally.
Supplemental Analysis — Ionosphere

Ionospheric TEC — Post-1998 Events

IONEX global ionosphere maps (2-hour resolution, JPLG product) were pulled from the NASA CDDIS archive for four events with available coverage: Nimitz 2004, O'Hare 2006, MH370 2014, and Stephenville 2008. TEC was interpolated to each event's coordinates and scored against a daily baseline. The Phoenix 1997 event predates the IGS ionosphere product, which began routine production in June 1998.

No TEC anomaly exceeded the 2.0σ threshold during any event window across any of the four events.

RESULT: Ionospheric electron density was undisturbed during all four event windows. No TEC anomaly was detected at any event location. IMPLICATION: The magnetometer anomalies are not associated with broad ionospheric disturbances. This is consistent with the spatial extent finding -- the signal appears to be a localized magnetic field perturbation rather than an ionospheric or space weather phenomenon. The Kp gate independently reaches the same conclusion via a different instrument type.

Note: 2-hour IONEX resolution means a brief transient event shorter than approximately 90 minutes could fall between map epochs. This analysis cannot rule out a short-duration ionospheric event coinciding precisely with the magnetometer window.

Event 05 — Control Case

MH370 — 2014-03-07

Added as a control case on the assumption that a missing aircraft would produce no magnetometer signal. Nine anomalies across three independent stations clustered at or within 5 minutes of 17:21 UTC — the exact minute the transponder went offline.

Previously unreported. This result was unexpected. Cross-station correlation (0.4452) is substantially lower than the UAP events, suggesting a different mechanism or a more localized disturbance. The finding is preliminary and has not been independently reviewed.

Pattern Analysis

Two Distinct Signal Types

TypeCharacteristicsEventsrLag
Type 1
Simultaneous regional		 Independent stations respond together across 1,000+ km at near-zero lag. High correlation. Spatially confined. Phoenix, Nimitz, O'Hare >0.970–1 min
Type 2
Propagating wavefront		 Sequential station peaks. Velocity tracks event geometry. Lower correlation. JAL 1628, MH370 0.44–varmin–hrs

Phoenix, Nimitz, and O'Hare produced r > 0.97 at near-zero lag across three separate events on three separate dates spanning nine years. The spatial analysis confirms the Type 1 signal is geographically bounded — not a global or continental-scale phenomenon.

WHAT THE DATA SHOWS FOR THE TYPE 1 EVENTS: — Produced a simultaneous magnetic field response across 1,000+ km — Was confined spatially to a regional corridor (six distant stations flat) — Left no detectable ionospheric signature — Occurred during geomagnetically quiet conditions (Kp < 3.0) on all three dates — Reproduced the same pattern on three independent dates across nine years WHAT THIS RULES OUT: — Global geomagnetic storm (spatial extent analysis, TEC analysis, Kp gate) — Solar wind interaction (Kp < 3.0 on all three dates) — Broad ionospheric disturbance (TEC flat at all four testable events) — Single-station instrument artifact (two-station lockstep, independent systems) The mechanism producing simultaneous identical variation across 1,000+ km in a spatially bounded corridor during quiet solar conditions on three independent dates is not identified.
Limitations

Constraints on These Findings

  • Speed estimates come from witness timestamps, not instrument tracking. Uncertainty is high and they should be treated as rough figures only.
  • Magnetometer correlation shows two stations moved together. It identifies a shared signal — it does not identify the source.
  • Pre-2008 NEXRAD archive is inaccessible. No radar cross-reference is possible for Phoenix, Nimitz, or JAL 1628.
  • IONEX resolution is 2 hours. A brief ionospheric transient could fall between map epochs and remain undetected.
  • The Kp solar gate (threshold 3.0) eliminates major geomagnetic storms but does not rule out all geomagnetic variation.
  • Stephenville 2008: r = 0.9711 but Kp was 3.333 (active). Finding is inconclusive.
  • MH370 result is preliminary and has not been independently reviewed.
  • Spatial extent analysis used 1-minute INTERMAGNET data. A highly localized or brief effect might not propagate to distant stations regardless of its nature.
  • This is a single-researcher open-source project. All findings should be treated as preliminary pending independent replication.
