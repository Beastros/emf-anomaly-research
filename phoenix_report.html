Method

Anomaly Convergence Score (ACS)

For each event, sensor data is retrieved from public APIs across a ±2-hour window. Each stream is scored against a rolling 20-minute baseline using a sigma deviation metric. A solar gate (GFZ Kp index) down-weights periods of elevated geomagnetic activity. Cross-station Pearson correlation identifies whether independent stations responded together.

A convergence event is logged when two or more independent sensor streams exceed the 2.0σ threshold within a ±10-minute window.

ACS(t) = ∑ [ wₙ × σₙ(t) × G(Kp) × N(s,t) ] σₙ(t) = |x - mean(20-min window)| / std(20-min window) G(Kp) = 1.0 if Kp < 3.0 (solar ELIMINATED) 0.2 if Kp ≥ 3.0 (solar POSSIBLE) N(s,t) = 1 − (files_present / expected) within coverage radius Weights : magnetometer 1.0 | NEXRAD 0.8 | Kp index 0.6 Threshold: σ > 2.0 to flag anomaly

Cross-station propagation velocity is estimated as station_separation / time_lag at peak Pearson r.

Event 01

Phoenix Lights — 1997-03-13

Finding 01 — Formation track

Eight witness-reported track segments. Four exceed the A-10 Warthog maximum speed of 706 km/h.

Witness timestamps and reported positions allow a rough track reconstruction across approximately 560 km from Paulden to Tucson, AZ. Speed estimates carry significant uncertainty due to imprecise timestamps.

Finding 02 — Two-station lockstep

Two magnetometer stations 1,013 km apart moved in near-perfect synchrony during the event window.

USGS magnetometers at Tucson, AZ (TUC) and Boulder, CO (BOU) are independent instruments on separate data acquisition systems. During the Phoenix Lights window their D-component traces show a Pearson correlation of 0.9704 at a lag of −1 minute. The implied propagation velocity exceeds 60,000 km/h, inconsistent with known atmospheric mechanisms.

Pattern Analysis

Two Distinct EM Signatures

TypeCharacteristicsEventsrLag
Type 1
Simultaneous regional		 Stations respond together across 1,000+ km. Near-zero lag. High r. Phoenix, Nimitz, O'Hare >0.970–1 min
Type 2
Propagating wavefront		 Sequential station peaks. Velocity tracks event geometry. JAL 1628, MH370 0.44–varmin–hrs

Phoenix, Nimitz, and O'Hare all produced r > 0.97 at near-zero lag across three separate events on three separate dates. The physical mechanism producing simultaneous identical variation across 1,000 km during geomagnetically quiet periods is not identified.

Limitations

Constraints on These Findings

  • Speed estimates come from witness timestamps, not instrument tracking. Uncertainty is high.
  • Magnetometer correlation shows two stations moved together. It does not identify the cause.
  • Pre-2008 NEXRAD data is inaccessible. No radar cross-reference is possible for Phoenix, Nimitz, or JAL 1628.
  • The Kp gate (threshold 3.0) eliminates major storms but does not rule out all geomagnetic variation.
  • Stephenville 2008: r = 0.9711 but Kp was 3.333 (active). Inconclusive.
  • MH370 result is preliminary and has not been independently reviewed.
  • Single-researcher open-source project, not an institutional study.
